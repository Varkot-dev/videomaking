# One Idea, One Minute, Visibly Authored

The educational Shorts that work in this niche are narrow: one self-contained object per video, 45 to 60 seconds, opening directly on the question, and attached to a named human who credits sources. The closest model to this pipeline, 3Blue1Brown's 2026 puzzle Shorts, runs 0:44 to 1:02, states one puzzle, withholds the answer, and draws 290K to 840K views each ([prepublish.ai](https://prepublish.ai/youtube-channel/3blue1brown)). YouTube does not ban synthetic voices or programmatic animation; its monetization rule targets content that "looks like it's made with a template with little to no variation across videos", and 2026 enforcement was automated, channel-level and error-prone enough to flag Kurzgesagt ([nofacetube](https://nofacetube.com/guides/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy/), [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/youtube-mistakenly-penalizes-popular-science-channel-kurzgesagt-for-ai-generated-slop-2000722702)). Learning research says a Short teaches when it cuts everything off-concept, makes the viewer commit to a guess, and refutes the tempting wrong answer, which is a different target from smoothness. For the pipeline this means a native vertical mode (not a crop of the landscape output), a script template built around a question and one insight, a voice other than the current stock edge-tts voice, deliberate variation across uploads, and a human review gate on every video. The evidence is uneven: the researchers could not open most pages or watch a single Short, so policy wording is second-hand, retention numbers are mostly vendor folklore, and every second-by-second description of what top Shorts look and sound like is the researchers' recollection, not observation. No verified example was found of a channel thriving with programmatic animation plus TTS narration, so the owner would be testing that combination, not copying it.

## How to read the evidence labels

Four labels recur below and in the requirements table. **Strong** means an official platform statement, an independent meta-analysis, or source code the researchers read directly. **Moderate** means a single study, tracker data, or official wording reproduced by a secondary outlet. **Weak** means vendor blogs and practitioner advice with no checkable method. **Inference** means the researchers' reasoning or arithmetic, and **unverified recall** means prior knowledge that was not confirmed against any source in this research. One caveat applies almost everywhere: apart from ManimGL source files and two GitHub READMEs, findings were taken from search-result summaries, not full pages. Quoted policy text should be checked at the YouTube Help pages for [channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392) and [altered or synthetic content disclosure](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491) before any decision depends on it.

## The proven format is a single question in under a minute

Tracker data gives one clean dataset. 3Blue1Brown's seven 2026 puzzle Shorts follow a fixed title template ("The ladybug clock puzzle", "The lattice bacteria puzzle"), run 44 to 62 seconds, and state the puzzle only ([prepublish.ai](https://prepublish.ai/youtube-channel/3blue1brown)). Grant Sanderson explained why in June 2026: "Puzzles are perhaps the only type of math content where I feel short-form videos are better than long-form videos. It's nice to concisely hear the statement, but you don't want the answer spoiled too soon" ([3Blue1Brown Substack](https://3blue1brown.substack.com/p/on-monthly-puzzles)). He is moving solutions into longer videos grouped by problem-solving tactic. The most successful Manim animator, in other words, uses Shorts to carry the question and long-form to carry the explanation. That maps directly onto a pipeline that already produces long-form: the Short is the hook for a video the pipeline can also make.

Within that dataset, **puzzles named after tangible things drew two to three times the views of puzzles named after mathematical objects**: bacteria 838.7K, ladybug 757.1K, phone number 742K, against subset sum 433.6K and sphere intersection 286K ([prepublish.ai](https://prepublish.ai/youtube-channel/3blue1brown)). Seven data points of different ages make this suggestive, not established. The bacteria puzzle's solution video (1.2M) out-viewed its own statement, so a paired question-then-answer series is worth testing despite Sanderson's own retreat from solution Shorts.

Three other formats have sourced support. Mathematical Visual Proofs (about 260K subscribers) uses ManimGL to animate one identity at one concrete value of n, often with music and no narration, and credits the original proof with a journal citation in every description ([channel page](https://www.youtube.com/@MathVisualProofs/shorts), [TikTok description](https://www.tiktok.com/@mathvisualproofs/video/7371959374060801322)). Andy Math (about 544K) solves one geometry figure per video and closes with a catchphrase ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UC6KhU3AMLHC-qv7tj0ZJduw/), [fandom wiki](https://youtube.fandom.com/wiki/Andy_Math)). Fireship (about 4.3M) compresses one named technology into roughly 100 seconds ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@fireship/), [YesPress](https://yespress.io/jeff-delaney)). The common rule is one object per Short.

Expectations should stay modest. On established channels Shorts earn fewer views per upload than long videos: 3Blue1Brown's long-form median is about 1.71M, and Kurzgesagt averages about 1.3M per Short against 4.9M per regular video ([prepublish.ai](https://prepublish.ai/youtube-channel/3blue1brown), [HypeAuditor](https://hypeauditor.com/youtube/UCsXVk37bltHxD1rDPwtNM8Q/)). Recent Mathematical Visual Proofs uploads listed on the Manim showcase sit at 3.7K to 10K views ([manim.community](https://www.manim.community/awesome/)). Text-to-Manim tools are now marketed to creators, so the visuals alone no longer differentiate a channel ([animg.app](https://animg.app/en/manim-for-youtube)).

What the researchers could not establish matters as much. The opening words, caption styling, music and endings of these Shorts were never observed. Descriptions such as "opens on the puzzle object already on screen, no greeting, the creator's own calm voice, minimal on-screen text" are unverified recall and need confirming by watching five Shorts per channel before they become specification. Authorship signals that are sourced are a named person behind the channel, a recurring series with a stable title pattern, credited sources, a verbal signature, and a link to a longer treatment.

## Retention has two gates and almost no reliable numbers

YouTube exposes the metrics that matter but not how it weighs them. Studio reports how many viewers chose to view a Short versus swiped away, and average percentage viewed; YouTube publishes no thresholds, weights or test-batch sizes ([outlierkit, citing YouTube Help](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)). Since 31 March 2025 a view counts on every play or replay with no minimum watch time, so raw views are inflated relative to older Shorts; the stricter count survives as "engaged views", which still drives earnings ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted)). The pipeline's owner should judge uploads on viewed-versus-swiped, average percentage viewed and engaged views, never on the public counter.

The only benchmark with a stated method is Paddy Galloway's April 2023 study of 5,400 Shorts: those with under 60% viewed-versus-swiped rarely performed, and the best sat between 70 and 90% ([Galloway on X](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1646898368495382528)). He called the results initial, and they predate both the three-minute limit and the view-counting change. Use 70% as a working target and treat it as a heuristic.

Length follows from the same study and the creator data. Shorts can run to three minutes if square or vertical ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/03/youtube-takes-on-tiktok-with-longer-shorts-templates-trends-and-more)), but no performance data was found for the 60 to 180 second range, and Galloway's finding was that holding viewers matters more than length itself ([Galloway on X](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1646898363466481664)). Together with the 44 to 62 second 3Blue1Brown range, **30 to 60 seconds is the defensible default**. Staying at or under 60 seconds also avoids a hard rule: a Short over one minute with any active Content ID claim is blocked globally ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)).

Everything else in the retention canon is weak. Hook formulas come from tool vendors with no controlled comparison; the consistent principle is to state the value or question before the first sentence ends, with no logo, greeting or title card ([OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/youtube-shorts-hook-formulas)). Pacing figures of "a visual change every 1.5 to 4 seconds" conflict across vendors and have no dataset behind them ([OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention), [aibrify](https://aibrify.com/blog/short-form-video-editing-captions-b-roll-guide)). Loop advice is practitioner opinion, and YouTube has confirmed no ranking weight for rewatches. The one item with data is cadence: Metricool's 2026 dataset found individual Shorts perform best at two to four uploads per week ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/03/youtube-shorts-long-form-study-metricool/)), and YouTube's Shorts lead said in 2023 that posting more gives no algorithmic boost ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-advice-for-shorts-creators-hashtags-algorithm/691835/)).

## Teaching in sixty seconds means adding effort, not polish

The learning literature is the strongest evidence in this report, and it complicates the retention folklore. Independent meta-analyses support cutting extraneous material and cueing where to look: signaling yields g = 0.53 on retention and 0.33 on transfer across 103 studies, smaller than Mayer's own lab medians ([ACU summary](https://inspire.acu.edu.au/articles/signalling)). Worked examples in mathematics give g = 0.48 ([Barbieri et al. 2023](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10648-023-09745-1.pdf)), and stating then refuting a misconception gives g = 0.41 ([Schroeder & Kucera 2022](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10648-021-09656-z.pdf)). Asking a question before the content improved learning from video in one study, though the effect weakened in authentic settings ([BPS Research Digest](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/heres-simple-way-boost-your-learning-videos-prequestion), [Toftness et al.](https://pure.royalholloway.ac.uk/files/30437641/JARMAC_Toftness_et_al.pdf)). Curiosity improves memory for the thing a viewer is curious about ([Gruber et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4252494/)).

These converge on a script shape, which is an inference from the studies and has not been tested in a sub-three-minute social video: open with a question the viewer must guess at, show one concrete worked case before the general statement, name and refute the tempting wrong answer, and end on a "what would happen if" prompt instead of a recap. The hook and the teaching device become the same thing, provided the curiosity gap is the concept itself. A hook about something tangential is exactly the extraneous detail the coherence research says to cut. A misconception must also be flagged as wrong and refuted at once, because many viewers leave in the first seconds and would otherwise carry the wrong claim away.

The uncomfortable finding is that felt clarity is not learning. Fluent delivery raised students' confidence without raising what they learned ([Carpenter et al. 2013](https://typeset.io/pdf/appearances-can-be-deceiving-instructor-fluency-increases-40w2tynmko.pdf)), and Derek Muller's research found that misconception-first physics videos, rated as more confusing, produced more conceptual change than clear expositions ([THE Journal](https://thejournal.com/articles/2015/05/05/watching-videos-does-not-necessarily-lead-to-learning.aspx?Page=1)). Whether Shorts teach at all is unsettled: one experiment with 123 participants found short videos produced more surface learning and lower quiz scores than the same content as text, while a 62-student trial found reels beat a long video on immediate recall ([Otto 2025](https://www.commbox.org/document/should-educators-be-concerned-the-impact-of-short-videos-on-rational-thinking-and-learning-a-comparative-analysis), [arXiv 2509.05962](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05962v1)). Both are small and test immediately. The honest claim is that a Short can produce immediate recall of one idea; nothing shows durable retention. "So clear!" comments are a poor proxy for learning.

Narration speed is not the constraint. Speech comprehension holds until about 275 words per minute for young adults ([Murphy et al. 2023](https://castel.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/111/2023/04/The-effect-of-video-playback-speed-on-learning-and-mind-wandering-in-younger-and-older-adults.pdf)), but those studies used lectures, not dense animation. The researchers' inferred budget is 150 to 170 words for 60 seconds, one visual change per narrated statement, and a beat of silence before and after the key reveal. No study gives an "ideas per minute" figure or an optimal rate of visual change.

### Captions are where retention advice and learning research collide

Vendors claim word-by-word animated captions raise watch time by 10 to 34%, with no independent study behind any figure ([blitzcutai](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/animated-captions-vs-static-captions), [dev.to](https://dev.to/clamper_ai/how-ai-caption-styles-affect-watch-time-and-engagement-409i)). The learning research points the other way for this format. A meta-analysis found that the effect of duplicating narration as on-screen text depended on pacing and on whether animation was present, while a few on-screen key words were associated with better learning ([Adesope & Nesbit 2012](https://rex.libraries.wsu.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Verbal-Redundancy-in-Multimedia-Learning-Environments/99900601157101842)). Mayer's own estimate of the harm fell from d = 0.87 to 0.10 between editions, so the current reading is that verbatim text "does not help", not that it hurts ([Chartered College summary](https://my.chartered.college/impact_article/how-multimedia-can-improve-learning-and-instruction/)). Second-language viewers are the exception and do benefit from text ([Lee & Mayer 2018](https://pure.uos.ac.kr/en/publications/fostering-learning-from-instructional-video-in-a-second-language/)). No study tests karaoke-style captions on a learning outcome, and the widely repeated "85% watch muted" figure has no primary source for Shorts.

The position this report takes: put one to four key terms or the equation on screen next to the thing they describe, upload a closed-caption track that viewers control, and treat burned-in word-by-word captions as an A/B variant to be judged on the channel's own analytics. The pipeline already has per-word timestamps from its TTS stage, so both a caption file and the variant are cheap to build.

## YouTube penalises sameness, and the pipeline's defaults are sameness

The policy position, as relayed by secondary outlets, is consistent. The "inauthentic content" rule, renamed on 15 July 2025, covers "mass-produced or repetitive content", including content that is "easily replicable at scale" ([nofacetube](https://nofacetube.com/guides/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy/), [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/)). It is a monetization rule assessed at channel level; removals happen under the separate spam policy ([Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/08/youtube-targets-inauthentic-content/)). YouTube said channels using AI tools remain eligible, and the disqualifying examples are defined by absent value: slideshows or scrolling text "with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value" ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755/), [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/entertainment/youtube/never-fear-reaction-videos-are-still-allowed-under-youtubes-new-inauthentic-content-policy-222401009.html)). Original code-generated animation with an original script sits on the permitted side in principle. No YouTube statement addressing TTS narration or programmatic animation specifically was found; "TTS is fine" rests on blogs interpreting the general rule.

Enforcement is the real exposure. Sixteen large AI channels were removed or wiped in January 2026, and a Google paper covered in July 2026 describes an automated system that terminated about 130K channels in clusters over six months ([Boing Boing](https://boingboing.net/2026/01/28/slop.html), [Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/artificial-intelligence/how-google-machine-terminated-130000-ai-slop-youtube-channels-six-months-903645)). Human faceless creators were caught as collateral, and Kurzgesagt reported being wrongly flagged around 31 July 2026 ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage), [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/youtube-mistakenly-penalizes-popular-science-channel-kurzgesagt-for-ai-generated-slop-2000722702)). The researchers infer that because classification is automated and looks at channels and clusters, structural and visual sameness across uploads is the main risk regardless of who wrote the script. A pipeline's natural output shares layout, palette, pacing, structure and voice, which is the pattern the rule describes.

That creates a tension with the creator evidence, where a stable identity is part of what makes a channel feel authored. The resolution is to hold constant the things a human brand holds constant (voice, palette, typography, a sign-off) and vary the things a template would not: format archetype, structure, length, layout, visual treatment and whether there is music. Four archetypes have support above: puzzle statement, misconception refutation, wordless or near-wordless visual proof, and a worked example. Because even a hand-made house style tripped the detector, evidence of human input belongs outside the video too: credited sources in each description, a channel page describing the process, and a kept record of script edits and review for appeals. Blog accounts say rushed appeals are bulk-denied and a failed appeal means a 90-day wait ([subsub.io](https://www.subsub.io/blog/inauthentic-content-why-so-many-creators-are-getting-demonetized)).

### The voice is the most recognisable tell

Vendor surveys name voice as a leading cue: 83% of consumers in one 460-person survey said they can spot AI video, citing unnatural voice patterns and flat emotional tone, and 77% in another said they trust human voices most ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260121875037/en), [Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/ai-audio-consumer-trust)). These are brand-video surveys, not studies of educational Shorts. Academic work finds the cost of a synthetic voice is engagement and social presence, not comprehension: AI-generated instructional video matched recorded video on learning while scoring lower on presence, and a modern TTS engine was mostly indistinguishable from a human recording on learning outcomes ([Xu et al. 2024](https://www.tiffin.edu/wp-content/uploads/Article-From-Recording-to-AI-Instructional-Video.pdf), [Craig & Schroeder 2019](https://searchworks.jioinstitute.edu.in/articles/eric__EJ1225297)). In a feed where the viewer decides in a second or two, engagement is the binding constraint. A Kapwing sample found 21% of the first 500 Shorts served to a new account were AI slop, so viewers are primed to pattern-match ([Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/blog/ai-slop-report-the-global-rise-of-low-quality-ai-videos/)). No data ties viewer drop-off to any specific TTS voice.

The pipeline currently narrates with edge-tts and the stock `en-US-AndrewMultilingualNeural` voice. The researchers infer that default library voices are the weakest choice on perception grounds and a shared fingerprint with channels being terminated; nothing was retrieved on edge-tts quality, perception or licence terms, and the caveat that edge-tts is an unofficial client of a consumer endpoint is unverified recall that needs checking before monetized use. Cost does not constrain the alternative: a 60-second script is roughly 900 characters, under $0.10 even at about $100 per million characters, by the researchers' arithmetic from blog-quoted prices ([besttts.org](https://besttts.org/compare)). In rising order of distinctiveness the options are a non-default licensed voice, a designed custom voice, a clone of the owner's own voice, and the owner recording narration. The last removes the question entirely and costs about a minute of speech per Short.

Disclosure guidance is unresolved. Realistic altered or synthetic content must be disclosed, cloning someone else's voice is a named example, and clearly unrealistic animation and AI-assisted scripts are reported as exempt ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491), [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-introduces-mandatory-disclosure-for-ai-content/)). Guides disagree on whether a generic synthetic narrator needs a label ([nofacetube](https://nofacetube.com/rules/youtube-altered-synthetic-content-disclosure/), [layer3labs](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/youtube-ai-content-policy)). Labelling voluntarily is the conservative choice.

Script tells are softer evidence. A detection vendor reports triads about four times more common in AI text, and commentators list the "it's not X, it's Y" contrast and noncommittal hedging ([Pangram](https://www.pangram.com/signs-of-ai-writing), [Dead Language Society](https://www.deadlanguagesociety.com/p/rhetorical-analysis-ai)). No marker is reliable and vocabulary shifts with each model generation, so a blocklist lags. What survives into audio is structure and even rhythm; the fix the sources support is specificity, a committed claim, and a human reading the script aloud before synthesis.

## Vertical output is a redesign of every scene

The delivery spec is the best-sourced part of production. A Short is a square or vertical video up to three minutes ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)); the recommended encode is 1080x1920 H.264 High Profile in MP4 with AAC-LC audio at 48 kHz ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171?hl=en)). ManimGL's writer already emits libx264 and yuv420p ([default_config.yml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/3b1b/manim/master/manimlib/default_config.yml)). YouTube's loudness reference of -14 LUFS comes from third parties; it turns loud content down and does not turn quiet content up, so the mix should be normalised to about -14 LUFS with -1 dBTP before upload ([Production Advice](https://productionadvice.co.uk/youtube-loudness/)).

In ManimGL 1.7.2 the frame follows the resolution: `FRAME_WIDTH = FRAME_HEIGHT * ASPECT_RATIO`, so rendering with `-r 1080x1920` at the default frame height of 8 gives a frame **4.5 units wide instead of 14.2** ([constants.py at v1.7.2](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/3b1b/manim/v1.7.2/manimlib/constants.py), [config.py at v1.7.2](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/3b1b/manim/v1.7.2/manimlib/config.py)). This was read from source and not test-rendered. The consequences are inferences but follow directly: any landscape scene wider than about 3.5 units overflows, and scaling a wide number line down shrinks its labels below legibility, so wide visuals must be rebuilt as short ranges, portrait axes, stacked equations and top-over-bottom comparisons. Setting `frame_height: 16` gives a 9 by 16 unit frame at 120 pixels per unit, which keeps existing font sizes roughly valid and is the gentler migration for prompts tuned to landscape. Three pitfalls matter for this codebase. The frame constants are computed at import, so the vertical config must be in place before `manimlib` loads. The `-l`, `-m`, `--hd` and `--uhd` flags override the resolution with landscape presets, and `manimgen/config.yaml` currently sets `rendering.quality: hd`, so that mapping needs checking. The assembler normalises to `1920x1080` from the same config, so the render flag, the ffmpeg scale and pad filters and the crossfade stage must switch together.

Safe zones have no official figure for organic Shorts. The most-cited set, traced to Google's vertical ads template, reserves 288 px at the top, 672 at the bottom, 48 on the left and 192 on the right, leaving an 840x960 clear box; looser reverse-engineered estimates block only 120 to 180 px at the top and 300 to 390 at the bottom ([revid.ai](https://www.revid.ai/youtube-shorts-safe-zone), [adkit.so](https://adkit.so/tools/safe-zones/youtube), [AdaptlyPost](https://adaptlypost.com/en/blog/social-media-safe-zones-2026-complete-guide), [flowshorts.app](https://flowshorts.app/blog/youtube-shorts-dimensions)). The sensible reading is two tiers: text and results that must never be covered go in the conservative box, and the animation itself can use roughly x 60 to 960 and y 180 to 1500. By the researchers' arithmetic, at frame height 8 the conservative box spans x from -2.05 to +1.45 and y from -1.2 to +2.8, so the visual centre of a Short sits above and left of the origin. The only way to settle the numbers is to upload an unlisted test Short carrying a pixel grid and look at it on a phone.

Text size guidance is vendor-level: a 48 px floor and 60 to 75 px for meaningful captions on a 1080x1920 canvas, with weight mattering more than size ([blitzcutai](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/best-caption-size-youtube-shorts-2026)). ManimGL's `font_size_for_unit_height: 144` lets the pipeline compute this: at 240 pixels per unit, font size 36 renders a digit about 60 px tall.

Layout is where automated Manim pipelines fail, and vertical makes it harder. TheoremExplainAgent scored 0.61 on element layout against 0.89 on logical flow, with misaligned text and overlapping shapes in most videos ([arXiv 2502.19400](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19400v2)), and a later paper calls spatial layout "a fundamental bottleneck in LLM-based animation" ([arXiv 2605.15585](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.15585)). All of these systems target landscape long-form. The inference is that a frame one third as wide, with model priors that are almost entirely landscape, will fail more often, so layout should be planned into fixed bands before code generation and checked deterministically against the safe box. The pipeline's vision layout check currently runs only on retries, per `manimgen/CLAUDE.md`; a vertical mode is the case for running it on the first pass.

Sound guidance is practitioner-level. Music sits 18 to 24 dB under the narration, effects are sparse and matched to motion, and stacking a whoosh on every move reads as noise on a phone speaker ([overseeros](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/background-music-volume-under-voiceover), [fish.audio](https://fish.audio/blog/using-sound-effects-for-short-form-content)). YouTube Audio Library tracks do not receive Content ID claims ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13486873?hl=en)). No data was found on whether top educational Shorts use music or whether it affects retention. One advantage is specific to this pipeline: animation timings are known in code, so an effect can land exactly on the frame where a transform completes.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of the project in two ways. First, the Short is not a compressed lesson. The creator evidence and the learning evidence agree that the format carries one question and one insight well and a derivation badly, so the right architecture is a Short that poses or cracks one idea and points to the long-form video the pipeline already knows how to make. Second, the largest risks are not technical. A vertical render mode is a bounded engineering task, while the policy and perception risks come from properties that automation produces by default: uniform structure, a stock voice, even rhythm and high cadence. The requirements below therefore put a human in the loop on purpose, as the source of the variation, opinion and fact-checking that both viewers and YouTube's classifiers appear to read as authorship.

The first experiments should close the gaps this research could not: watch and transcribe five Shorts from each model channel, upload a pixel-grid test to measure the real safe zone, read the live policy pages, and run the caption and voice choices as A/B tests on the channel's own viewed-versus-swiped and average-percentage-viewed numbers. Until then, most thresholds in the table are starting values.
